# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The weapon-balance bars on ship and fleet tooltips now name each side the way the ship design screen does — "Projectile 100%", "Projectile 60%, Energy 40%" — instead of a ship's bare percentages ("100% to 0%") whose order you had to infer from the "Projectile-Energy Balance" heading, and a fleet's saying nothing at all. The bar splits offensive power between projectile (kinetic and missile) and energy (laser and beam) weapons; the defensive bar splits absorption between hull plating and shields the same way. A side worth nothing stays unspoken, matching the game hiding its half of the bar
- The fleet panel's first two stops now match the constructibles shape. The Fleets stop keeps the buttons that act on the selection — select all, merge, and the rest — in an "Actions" section on one row, with the fleets below it one per line; the Ships stop holds the ship toolbar in an "Actions" section, the ships one per line, and, when a hero is aboard, a "Hero" section before both. `Tab` into either stop now lands on the first fleet or the first ship — what you came for — with the buttons one `Alt+Up` away. Another empire's fleets keep the game's own "belongs to another empire" sentence above the list, with no empty sections around it
- The game now tells you about things it only ever showed on the map, spoken once as each happens: a new star system discovered (named with its map position), a foreign fleet or colony sighted, a foreign fleet slipping out of view ("last seen at…") or moving between systems over the end of a turn, your own fleets arriving — or being stopped short by an interception — cargo ships automatically dispatched from your systems, your systems coming under siege or blockade, a trade blockade lifting, and Obliterator fire
- The Turn log: those notifications collect in a panel of their own, right after Notifications in the top bar of every screen, grouped under "Turn 21", "Turn 20" headings with the newest turn first — so you always know when something happened without the notification having to say so. `Enter` opens one as a normal notification pop-up (its text starts "Turn 21:", so a reviewed item still says when it arrived), `Backslash` discards it, a Show Location button flies the camera there, and the pop-up's own Previous/Next browse across the game's notifications and the Turn log's alike. Entries discard themselves after five turns, none of this is ever written into a save game, and the game's own notification strip is unchanged on screen
- Minimizing a notification pop-up now puts you back on the list that notification lives in — the Turn log for a Turn log entry, Notifications for the game's — even when you browsed from one family to the other inside the pop-up
- A star system shared by several empires now says so in its details — "Colonies here:" followed by the empire names, your own empire first — reading the data behind the row of empire-colored bars the game draws under the label. Systems with a single owner are unchanged
- A special node whose exploration contest has been won now reads the game's own line about who discovered it, alongside the node's other badges
- Galaxy map: inspect mode names the constellation you are in — on entry when you start inside one, and while moving only when it changes: "Andromeda constellation" crossing in, "Out of Andromeda constellation" crossing into unclaimed space, spoken before the cell. Cells in unexplored fog read as before, with no constellation words. The regions are the mod's own reading of the map — the game draws no borders — derived from each constellation's member systems
- Galaxy map: the map is now grouped by constellation. The top level holds one entry per constellation you have explored, anything drifting in open space at its own position, and an "Unexplored constellation" group at the end for every discovered system whose constellation the game has not named to you yet. A constellation entry reads the game's name for it, and its details carry the game's own constellation information: who controls it, who fully discovered it, your progress toward control ("You control 1/3 Star Systems needed to own this constellation"), and the control bonus. Opening a constellation never moves the camera; collapsing one whose system you had flown into zooms back out. The per-system "In the X constellation" line is gone — the grouping says it. Searches, locates and fleet-action landings all follow the deeper tree, opening groups as needed
- Anything that moves your reading position to something inside a collapsed system — a notification's Inspect, a quest locate, the scanner's jump, a fleet action's landing — now opens the branch and lands on the thing, announced once, instead of silently failing. Your own next key always wins: pressing an arrow while a landing is still on its way cancels it, so nothing yanks you back later
- Fleet actions that pick their target inside the system — Colonize, Super Colonize, Destroy Planet, Start Expedition, Launch Mining Probe, Reclaim Mothership — now say what they will do ("moves focus to the first curiosity in the system") and then do it: the system opens and the cursor lands on the first matching control, ready for `Enter` to give the real order. These actions never arm a targeting mode. When the system offers no such target, the branch opens and the cursor stays put
- Arming any targeting mode — from a fleet action or anywhere else — ends inspect mode first: "Exited inspect mode", then one line beginning with the game's instruction and running on into where you are standing, the instruction said exactly once. The six seat-and-go actions above end it the same way. `Ctrl+I` while a mode is armed still works: sweep to your target, `Enter` lands on it and exits inspect, and a second `Enter` confirms the mode there
- Galaxy map: `Enter` on a starlane whose far end you have explored now travels down it, exactly like `Right` — but only when nothing else wants the key: with a targeting mode armed it still confirms the target, and with a fleet selected it still puts the fleet down. A lane into unexplored space still does nothing
- Galaxy map: while an action is waiting for a target, the map panel names itself with the game's own instruction ("Left Click to launch a probe, Esc/ Right Click to cancel" and the like) instead of "Map" — so tabbing out and back re-reads what the game is waiting for
- The zoom announcement now ends with the name of the layer band the camera is on — "Zoom level 5 of 15, Constellation" — on the automatic announcements and on the zoom slider alike. The bands, from furthest out: Galaxy map, Informative galaxy, Constellation, Systems, System, System Overview; the very furthest step and the system/planet pages add no word. In the game's scan mode the band word stays quiet — the lens titles name the steps there
- Hero skills: the effects table in a skill's tooltip now reads level by level — "Level 1", then the situation it applies in ("On System (if assigned)"), then each effect on its own line. Before, the level number was glued onto the first effect ("1 +1 Industry per Population on Planets") and read like a quantity. A part-levelled skill reads its current level's block and then the next level's; starting skills, relic skills and senator skills read the same way
- The hero skill page opens on the skill wheel, and the wheel calls itself "Skill Tree" — the game's own heading — instead of the mod's "Skill branches". The heading-only tab stop before the wheel is gone; the overview and ship-design pages keep theirs
- The skill page's right-hand column is sections now: `Alt+Up` and `Alt+Down` jump between the skill stats, the starting skills, the masteries, and — on heroes that have one — the relics box
- A starting-skill row says its mastery — "Command", "Labor" — instead of every row reading "Starting Skill". The row's tooltip still carries the skill's effects
- Galaxy map: in inspect mode, `Shift+arrows` move to the next square in that direction that differs from where you stand — squares with the same contents and the same fog cover are skipped, and the mod says how many ("Skipped 12 squares") before reading the landing. When nothing differs all the way out, you land on the last square before the map edge
- Galaxy map: in inspect mode, `Alt+Left` and `Alt+Right` travel by what the cursor holds — to the first- or second-named end of a lone starlane, or, with `Alt+Right`, to the destination of the fleets crossing the square when the map shows one and they all agree (a single fleet's destination wins over the lane it is flying). An ambiguous or unexplored answer does nothing, and never ends the mode
- In inspect mode with a type-ahead search open, `Escape` now clears only the search; the next `Escape` exits the mode — matching how a search's Escape behaves everywhere else
- The construction-queue-empty pop-up's idle systems are now buttons: `Enter` opens that system's management view, as the mouse click does. They previously read as plain text and `Enter` did nothing
- The research-queue-empty pop-up now offers the game's suggested technologies as cards — technology, branch, cost, each a button that queues it — the same reading the research-complete pop-up already had. It previously read as twelve loose labels
- Launching probes from the fleet actions now lands you on the "Launch towards" group's north button, wherever you were reading — the direction picker no longer has to be found by walking back to the fleet's system
- The turn controls now read the in-game clock when you have it turned on (Options → "Display In-Game Clock") — "Clock, 5:50 PM", in whichever format you chose. It is read only when you land on it, never by itself
- In a scan view the game holds notification pop-ups back; a "Show pending notifications" button now appears among the turn controls when pop-ups are waiting — pressing it closes the scan view and the waiting pop-ups open. In the normal view the button never exists, and its arrival is not announced
- The colony panel's defense value is captioned "System Garrison" — the game's own word for it, matching the Hissho "Citadel Garrison" row — instead of the mod's "Security"
- The system management page's colony panel now reads everything the game can draw on it: the military status banner (besieged, blockaded, being invaded, being converted, frozen) with its explanation in the review buffer, the ownership percentage of a contested system, the resources the system exploits, wrecked arks, active temporary effects, a Vodyani ark's name (Enter renames it) and its Detach button, the ghost-system Decolonize tick box, the Hissho citadel garrison, and the three corner badges — home system, trading company, and cloaking level with who can see through it. Each appears only while the game draws it; on an ordinary system the panel gains just the home-system badge
- A table row the game refuses no longer says "unavailable" twice on the cells that carry their own refusal (the empire screen's action cells and the military screen's hero cells); every other cell keeps its single word
- Diplomacy: re-centring the ring in swap mode now announces "Viewing relations from the perspective of …" — on the way out to another empire, and on the way back to you when swap mode ends (Escape, right-click, or unticking Swap position). Opening the screen is unchanged, and the centre line stays re-readable as the first item of Empires
- Battle tactics: when changing your tactics deck costs influence, the window's "Empire Resources" strip is now read — your influence stock, with the full influence description in its review buffer. While the change is free the game draws no strip and there is nothing extra to walk. The Confirm button already reads its cost ("Confirm changes Cost: 50 Influence" or "Cost: FREE")
- The empire dossier — the "Empire Information" sheet on the introduction pop-up, diplomatic offers and the negotiation table — now reads every explained item on a line as its own entry. Where two items share a line, each carries its own explanation in the review buffer; before, only the first one's explanation was reachable
- Remove the use of unneeded columns everywhere to reduce confusion
- Ship design: add a way to remove modules, fixed confusing wording for installed modules, add labels to various panels
- The constructibles and hangar panels (star system and empire screens) are now labelled, with the constructible filters in a "Filters" section, the hangar's buttons in an "Actions" section, and every constructible or ship read one per row in an "Available" section
- The ship designer's module filters share one row again inside their "Filters" section, matching the constructibles pattern
- Mod manager: the mods library now reads as a "Filters" section holding the folder switches on one row, followed by an "Available" section with the library's contents one per row, matching the constructibles pattern
- Ship design: the ship's module slots are now read grouped by the type of module they take (defence, then support, then weapon), instead of wherever the hull happens to draw them. A slot that takes several types is read with the first of them, and what is fitted in a slot never changes where it is read
- Ship design: an empty slot now reads the three markers the game only draws as pictures — "Times 2 Multiplier" for the dots that multiply a fitted module, "Symmetrical (x2 cost)" for a slot mirrored on the far side of the ship, and "Heavy Mount" for the slot the game only draws bigger. A filled slot is unchanged: its module's own tooltip already says all three
- Ship design: the statistics panel's module health figure is now called "Health Bonus" instead of announcing only its explanation and a bare number
- Research screen: the branches and stages of the technology tree now say only their names ("Military", "Military I") — the researched-over-available counts in front of every group are gone
- Queuing now speaks, on both queues. On the star system and empire screens, queuing a construction says what went into the queue ("Queued Interplanetary Transport Network") instead of answering the key with silence, and the research screen answers with the same words ("Queued Survival Suits"). Queuing at the front says so ("Queued Infinite Supermarkets as first item"), and taking a line out — from either queue, or by re-pressing a technology's dot on the wheel — says "Cancelled Survival Suits"
- The Alt-click chord is `Ctrl+Shift+Enter`, not `Alt+Enter`. `Alt+Enter` is Unity's own fullscreen switch, below anything the mod can take a key off, so pressing it resized the game window as well as doing what you asked. `Ctrl+Shift+Enter` does everything the game gives an Alt-click — queuing a construction or a technology at the head of its queue, and every other place the game gives a click an Alt variant
- Economy screen: the luxury resources are now an eight column table, one column per resource family. The family names are the table's column headings and nothing you walk through: tabbing in lands on the first line of the table and says which family you are in, moving sideways says which family you have crossed into, and what a family does for a system's development is in the review buffer of every resource in its column. Moving up and down keeps the column, and a family with no resource on that line says "empty" so the columns stay lined up. Typing still finds a resource by name from any column
- The strategic resources (economy screen) and both component grids in the recipe dialog are the same table, read the same way — tabbing in lands on the first line and says which family you are in, sideways says which family you crossed into, up and down keeps the column, and a family with nothing on a line says "empty". Choosing a component in the recipe dialog is unchanged: Enter still puts it into the project, and Enter on a project slot still takes it back out. The recipe dialog's family names are fixed as well ("Industry" instead of "Improves Industry Food")
- Galaxy map: a probe in flight is now listed at the top level of the map, in the same section as obliterator missiles and ally pins, instead of being hidden inside whichever system it happened to be flying nearest to. Its row still says which star it is out from, which way and how many turns
- Galaxy map: tabbing between the panels now says which one you have landed in — "Map", "Quest", "Notifications" and "View Controls" (the zoom and scan controls). The quest and notification panels say their name on every screen that draws them
- Galaxy map: inspect mode (Ctrl+I) now opens on a single square instead of a three by three one — press plus to widen it. Pressing plus or minus when the cursor is already at its largest or smallest now says nothing at all, instead of repeating the size you are already on
- The top-left corner of every screen now says which of its rows you are on, and Alt+Up and Alt+Down jump between them: "Controls" for the strip of buttons that open the game's screens, "Key Resources" for dust, manpower and influence, "Research", "Strategic Resources" for the stockpiles, and — for the empires that have them — the faction rows "Essence", "Manage Population", "Singularities", "Golden Age", "Pirate Mark", "Keii" and "Relics"
- Notification pop-ups read their content first, and the controls are a Tab stop of their own, separate from what the notification says. Tab moves between reading the notification and working it — browsing to the next or previous one, popping this kind up automatically, minimizing and Done — so a long report is read to its end without the controls in the way, and they are one key away from anywhere in it
- Battle tactics: the two lists now say which one you have tabbed into — "Available" for the tactics you can pick up, and the game's own "Tactics" for the set below. The "4 tactics available" count row and the repeated "Tactics" caption row are gone
- Tutorial pop-ups are now a list of pages: moving up and down turns the game's own pages and reads them, so the previous/next page buttons are gone. The minimized bar is a labelled "Tutorial" panel reading its title, then Minimize, then Close
- Drawn captions across the senate family now label their sections instead of occupying rows, and the laws/population dialogs gained section jumps (Alt+Up/Down)
- The diplomacy screen now says whose ring of empires is shown (the centre empire is a readable line at the top of the list)
- The faction chooser's ship hull viewer is a list: moving through it shows and reads each hull, replacing the previous/next buttons
- The options screen's settings say which category tab they belong to; the DLC browser's list says which tab it is showing
- Changing a game setting now says the game's own sentence about the value you have moved to, not just its name — "Slow, A slow-paced game takes about 450 turns maximum" instead of "Slow". This covers the new game lobby, its advanced settings, and the settings panels in the pause menu; the options screen is unchanged, because its sliders and tick boxes keep one description whatever they are set to
- The pause menu names itself from the game's own drawn title
- The view title is no longer in the tab order anywhere: the galaxy map keeps the zoom and scan controls, other views keep just their scan button, and Escape remains the way to close a screen
- The screen title's close button is no longer in the tab order — press Escape to close a screen instead
- Review buffers: Ctrl+Left and Ctrl+Right now step over any buffer with nothing in it, so a single-player game no longer cycles through an empty chat log to get back to where it started
