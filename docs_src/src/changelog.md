# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Research screen: what a technology stage grants is under the stage now. An expanded stage — "Military I" — carries a "Tooltips" section with one entry per improvement or module the stage unlocks, each reading that unlock's own full page: its cost, its category, the paragraph the game wrote about it. The stage's own tooltip only lists the grants in brief; these are the pages behind it, the same treatment a technology's unlock icons already had
- Diplomacy: an empire you have not met says what the screen shows and nothing more — "Unknown Empire, unavailable, You can't negotiate with an unknown empire". The game fills every card's pressure trend, attitude and "Click to negotiate" footer with live figures and hides them only behind a hover it never grants an unknown or eliminated empire, so the mod was reading numbers no sighted player could see
- Galaxy map: "Next idle fleet" arrives in one move. The game's own button flies the camera to the fleet's docking slot first and only then selects it, after which the mod's landing moved the camera a second time. The mod's entry now takes the game's own cycle of idle fleets, lands your cursor on the fleet — the one camera move: its system's view for a docked fleet, its own point for one under way — and selects it with no flight
- Galaxy map: opening a system lands on what is really there. Expanding a system, or a search landing inside one, used to read the tree from the very frame the camera began arriving — before the map had drawn the close-up surface — so focus fell on the first planet instead of "Open system", a planet with a curiosity read as if it had nothing under it, and arrowing away and back "fixed" it. A landing inside a system now waits the few frames the map needs — around a tenth of a second — and says the settled truth once: expanding your home system reads "Open system, button, 1 of 10"
- Galaxy map: the map draws the label of the system you land in. The game only re-checks which labels to draw on frames the camera moved by its own hand, so a landing that snaps into a system left its name-plate undrawn — and with it the management button and a foreign home's Diplomacy button, whose entries were missing until something else stirred the camera. Every snap now re-arms the game's own refresh, so the label appears on screen and its entries are there the moment you arrive
- Galaxy map, inspect mode: a square says what is on it before where it is — "Probe, -79, 4" instead of "-79, 4, Probe" — and an empty square still reads the bare coordinate pair. Typing now does nothing while the mode is up: the letters start no search (Up and Down belong to the grid, so a search's results could never be walked) and still do not fall through to the game's own hotkeys; a search left open when you enter the mode is quietly cleared
- System management: another empire's colony in one of your systems reads its population too. An enemy outpost sitting on a free world of a system you hold gets a card among your planet cards, and the game draws that card's ring from their colony, with their people in it — the mod used to offer no population rows there at all, so the one card that could tell you how many of them live on your doorstep was the one card that said nothing. It now walks exactly like your own: a row per slot in the same bands, each filled slot naming their species and opening their dossier, each empty slot under the arc carrying the overpopulation sentence. Nothing there can be moved — their population is not yours to pick up, and their card is not a place to put yours down — which is what the game refuses too
- System management: a planet card reads its population ring as slots — one row per marker, in the game's own bands. The plain slots sit under "Population", the slots the orange arc covers under "Overpopulation" (the game's word for the approval penalty), and slots locked by a shrunken maximum under "Locked", so where the penalty starts is read the same way a sighted player reads the arc's edge, and region movement jumps between the bands. A filled slot names its people and shows their full dossier — drawn beside the ring itself, where the mouse would see it — a filled slot under the arc carries the game's overpopulation sentence as a nested Tooltips entry, and an empty slot under the arc reads that sentence directly. Before, the ring was one summary row per species ("Imperials, 3") and nothing said the planet's capacity or the penalty's threshold at all. Picking a unit up to move it is unchanged: Space on a filled slot, Enter on the destination card
- System management: a world nobody has settled reads its room the same way — the game draws its ring of empty markers on every card, so a colonizable planet now walks "Population, Empty slot, 1 of 6" through "6 of 6", which is the number a colonization is decided on. The row says only "Empty slot" because its place in the region already counts it
- System management: a planet's resource deposits say what they are. The card draws each deposit as an icon and a bare count, with the resource's name only on a hover card — the buffer read "3" and "2" where the screen shows titanium and hyperium. The lines now read "Titanium: 3", "Hyperium: 2", and each deposit's full card — its size, its paragraph, what it gives per population — is an entry in the card's Tooltips section
- System management: three more surfaces stop reading rows the game has faded away — the same retired-pool-row class the laws screen's fix covered. The side panel's population circles announced ghost buttons from a previously viewed system ("button, 2" with no name), a planet card could offer a nameless curiosity button left over from another world, and an outpost's action ticks could wear the names of a different outpost's actions seen earlier. All three tables now read only what the screen actually paints
- System management: Space stays on the page. The game's screen-level shortcut flips the view into scan mode, so pressing Space on any row that had nothing to pick up silently swapped the whole view out from under the keyboard. The key is now claimed for the entire screen — it picks a unit up where a slot offers that, and otherwise does nothing — and scan mode remains where its button is, on Enter
- Notification pop-ups: a hero offered for recruitment reads like a hero. The card used to be its labels in picture order — "Skills Mastery", four bare "0/6" figures, "Level" and "2" as separate lines, the ship's name floating alone — and the masteries' names were missing entirely, because they live on tooltips the old reading never opened. The card now reads the way the Academy reads one: name, affinity, class and politics, "XP, 0/100", "Level, 2", then "Skills Mastery" with every mastery named beside its own figure — "Wit, 0/6", "Labor, 0/18" — and the hero's ship last
- Hero cards: what a mouse learns by hovering inside the card is under the card now. A hero card — on the recruitment pop-up, the Academy's strip, the hero-selection window and the hero's inspection page — is an expandable entry whose Tooltips section holds the pages the card only shows on hover: the affinity's dossier, the class's, the politics party's, one page per mastery, the sentences explaining the experience gauge and the mastery block, and the hero's ship — its full design page with role, size, health, movement, the two military powers and both balance bars, which no keyboard could reach before
- Tooltips: a ship's two balance bars say which power each one splits. Both bars read out of the same two side words, so a ship whose weapons and defenses lean the same way said the identical line twice — "Projectile 100%" then "Projectile 100%" again, nothing telling weapons (kinetics and missiles against lasers and beams) from armor (hull plating against shields). Each line now leads with the game's own name for the figure it breaks down — "Offensive Power Projectile 100%", "Defensive Power Projectile 35%, Energy 65%" — on every surface that draws the ship page: a fleet's ship tiles, the ship designer's list, a hero's ship, a wrecked mothership. The designer's own two bars already announced their full sentences and are unchanged
- Galaxy map: `Ctrl+M` summarizes the map — one sentence saying what the galaxy was generated as, in the game's own setup words, how far across it is, and where its middle lies from home: "Spiral - 2 galaxy, Medium size, 187 by 130 units; galactic center 70 west, 22 north of Dusay." It speaks only while you are on the galaxy map, and the key is rebindable in Mod settings under "Summarize the map"
- Spoken offsets say their east-west component first everywhere now — "5 east, 13 north" — matching the order the coordinate pairs are already said in; the scanner's results used to put the north-south half first
- Galaxy map: a system somebody told you about — a curiosity's map reward, a map-sharing treaty, a hack, an enemy fleet spotted parked at an unknown star — is in the tree at last, as "Unexplored system" with its coordinates, exactly as the game draws it: an anonymous star with no name, no dossier and nothing to open. It sits in reading order under its constellation when the constellation has a name, and under "Unexplored space" — the renamed catch-all for everything standing where the map draws no constellation name — when it does not. A fleet you can see parked there is its one kind of child, Enter brings the camera in and Backslash takes it back out, and the scanner deliberately keeps not listing what the game's own scan view hides
- Galaxy map: launching a probe now offers sixteen compass headings instead of eight, and every heading says what is out there before the probe is spent — "Southeast: unexplored 11-26, 27-29, and 30 to the map edge at 40; unexplored alongside to the southwest: 1-3, 4-11, 26-27, and 29-30." The first stretches are the flight line itself, read off the game's own fog of war at the very tiles the inspect cursor samples, so every number can be walked to and checked; fog the probe would merely brush beside the line is kept apart and named by the side it lies on. A heading with nothing left to find says "fully explored to the map edge". The group announces the probe's reach once ("Launch probe, reach 30"), so a stretch beyond that number is one this probe will not get to, and the launch confirmation names the finer heading ("Probe launched heading north-northeast"). Star lanes and the scanner keep the eight-word compass: the finer words exist where an order is aimed, not where a picture is described
- Galaxy map: cancelling a probe launch gives the fleet panel back to the fleet that armed it. The game's own cancel re-selects the docking slot, whose panel then defaults to the first fleet parked there — so at a system holding two of your fleets, arming Launch Probes from one and pressing Escape reopened the panel for the other. The game's cancel is now finished rather than replaced, so the mouse's right-click cancel is repaired along with the keyboard's — and so are the two other ways the mode ends, the last probe being spent and the fleet being teleported. Cancelling while you stand among the launch bearings also seats the cursor on the fleet's own entry in the tree, since the bearings vanish with the mode; cancelling from anywhere else moves nothing
- Galaxy map: a full screen opened over the map no longer takes the fleet panel out from under you. Opening the Military screen — or any other full screen — clears the game's fleet selection, and the panel used to close while the mod's map page was away, leaving the cursor stranded on whatever row happened to survive, sometimes an unrelated fleet on the far side of the map. The release is now caught as the page steps aside and answered the moment it is back: the cursor lands on the closed panel's fleet, its system if the fleet is gone, and a cursor that was reading the HUD or the map stays exactly where it was
- Galaxy map: closing the fleet panel brings the camera back onto the system, immediately. Selecting a docked fleet makes the game frame the fleet itself, a whisker off its star — close enough to look like nothing happened, far enough that the system loses focus and its planets lose their buttons, so after an Escape the planet with a curiosity read as if the curiosity's button did not exist until some later key happened to move the camera. Two things changed underneath: the camera rule no longer believes its own record of where it sent the camera once anything else has moved it — the game leading you to a selected fleet, a landing sliding across open sky, the inspect cursor sweeping — and every way the mod places the cursor now asks that one rule, so a seat behaves exactly like an arrow key. A zoom you made by hand still survives reading the rest of the system
- Notification pop-ups: an event whose choice is settled no longer offers the cards the game faded away. The narrative event window retires its losing choice cards by fading them to nothing while leaving them marked visible, and the mod's drawn test exempted tables that keep counting a faded child's slot — so "Hear My Last Cry" read three radio buttons where the screen drew one, with the two phantoms answering as real choices. The drawn test now follows the game's renderer, which skips anything faded to nothing no matter how the table arranges it, so every surface built on pooled or faded rows — choice cards, law bands, quest journal rows, report tables — declares only what is actually painted
- Galaxy map: docked fleets are back in the scanner. The game moves a fleet out of the fleet-label repository and into its system's docking slot the moment it finishes docking, so the scanner's fleet categories and the inspect cursor's square only ever saw fleets under way — "go to the nearest fleet" skipped everything parked at a star. Both now read the two halves of what the map draws alike: the fleets in transit and the fleets docked at systems
- Galaxy map: a friendly minor civilization's fleets no longer announce as "Enemy Fleet". The game's own tooltip heading ranks every relation on the major-empire ladder and calls everything off that ladder — every minor faction, the Academy, the pirates — an enemy, while the picture only ever tints the lozenge in its owner's colour. The mod now picks between the game's four count phrases itself: a major empire keeps the game's ladder (a cold-war rival still reads as enemy, exactly as its tooltip does), a minor civilization and the Academy read by their actual standing — at war is enemy; brainwashed, aligned or integrated is allied; cordial, amicable, friendly or merely met is neutral — and a pirate fleet stays an enemy until you have bought peace
- Galaxy map: letting a fleet selection go while you stand in the fleet panel now puts the cursor on the fleet's own entry in the tree. The panel's three stops vanish with the selection, and the cursor used to be stranded on whatever row happened to survive nearby — typically a drifting probe on the far side of the map. Now "Fleet panel closed" is followed by the fleet's row under its system; deselecting a fleet you just disbanded lands on its system instead, and a deselection made while you are reading somewhere else still moves nothing
- Notification pop-ups: a quest's opening lore is back. The quest pop-up scrolls its lore paragraph and the objective's sentence together in one window, and when the paragraph's top edge poked out over the pop-up's title bar the mod measured it as part of the title strip and dropped it — the "Solo Quest Started" pop-up read only the one-line objective lore. A line partly showing through a scrolling window and partly cut off by it is now measured at the window wherever it is cut off, not only when the line alone outsizes it, so the pop-up reads the whole story first and the objective after it
- Galaxy map: a foreign system keeps its owner and its garrison at every zoom. Zoomed all the way into a system someone else holds, the game builds its own star card without the colony — the header lost its owner ("Osulo" instead of "Osulo - Niris") and the "Defense: 100% (Infantry 40)" line vanished from the system row's buffer, exactly where a player goes looking for a minor civilization's manpower. The mod now declines that thinner card wherever the map's label would bind a colony, so the row's tail and its Tooltips entry read the same full card — owner, star description, Defense — whether you are looking at the whole galaxy or standing inside the system, and the tooltip drawn on screen shows the same lines
- Planets nobody has settled rate their outputs — the five pip cells the game draws with no words beside them. "Planet Food production: 2 of 5, Low" up to "Planet Industry production: 5 of 5, Outstanding", in the game's own band names (None, Weak, Low, Medium, High, Outstanding), on the galaxy map's planet cards, the system management page and the empire screen's planet panel — the empire screen used to read numbers the game deliberately hides behind those pips. A colonized planet keeps its real numbers, and every output line now binds its title to its figure with a colon — "Planet Food production: 16" — on those surfaces and the planet page alike. The planet page's own card and the discovery scene already draw the band words as text, so the mod keeps reading the game's words there
- The laws screen reads exactly the selected law's effects. Selecting a law after one with more effects used to keep the leftovers — "Mine's Bigger Decree" read three effects where the game draws one, the other two belonging to the previously selected law. The same ghost-row fix covers every other list read out of a table the game refills: side-panel effect blocks, the minor-civilization window's effects and modifiers, a population's psychological traits, quest rewards, the senate's support lines, and the system view's temporary effects
- A law's experience bar reads as everything it draws. The requirement row still announces "Required Political experience: Potent Scientists", and its review buffer now holds the party's whole ladder — one line per tick on the bar, "Established: 20%", "Entrenched: 60%", "Potent: 100%" — so the requirement is a position on a scale, not a bare adjective. A law out of your reach gains a second row at the bar's notch: "Current Political experience: Established (20%)", with the game's own explanation ("This party has to win a few more elections for you to be able to vote that law.") in its buffer. The law's tooltip reads the same lines in drawn order; a law the party already qualifies for reads the requirement and the ladder, nothing more — exactly what the game draws
- An icon's word is only left unsaid when the same word is written right next to it. Before, it was dropped if the word appeared anywhere in the sentence — so the approval description lost "Influence" to the verb "influence" ("can influence the Food and outputs"); it now reads "…influence the Food and Influence outputs…", a pirate fleet heading loses its "Pirates Pirate Fleet" stutter, and three eaten units ("45 Dust", "-8.5 Food") come back
- Tooltips: the last caption-less cards say whose figures they draw. A fleet list's compact cards, its "+ 2 more fleets" footer and an automated delivery fleet caption their command points — "Command Point 1" instead of the figure gluing onto the fleet's name — and the compact card also names its health, its offensive and defensive power and its counts by hull size ("Small 1, Medium 0, Large 0, Ark 0"). A reinforcement list's small ship card reads like the full ship panel ("Role Exploration, Size Small, Movement 6, Command Points 1, Manpower 30..."), where it read "Colonizer 5 1" before. An invasion panel's manpower total says "Manpower 120" instead of opening the troop row as if there were 120 Infantry. The game's localizer turns an inline icon into a font glyph before the mod could name it, so a system's approval description read "can influence the and outputs" where the screen shows the Food and Influence symbols. - Ground battles: the setup pop-up now says the balance of power — "Manpower 160 against 200", the two numbers behind the dial the game draws with no number on it — and which side is attacking ("Your troops, Offensive Power, This player is the Attacker, he has offensive Tactics"). - Ground battles: the report pop-up's outcome announces the game's own meaning for the word ("Major Victory, You inflicted significantly more damage than the enemy"), the balance after the fight is a row ("Manpower 135 against 20"), the troops row is called "Remaining" as the game draws it there (it wrongly read the setup pop-up's "Assigned" before), each side's tactic explains itself with its full card — so the enemy's plan, which you never chose, reads its description and effects — and the damage gauges nothing spoke are rows: "Damage Dealt, Total Troop Damage applied: 2009", then one row per source, "Damage caused by bombardment: 1400", "Damage caused by your Infantry: 609"

A "Mod settings" entry sits just after Options on the main menu and on the pause menu (it is not drawn, but it is a stop like any other), and the window opens with two pages: Scanner and Controls
- Mod settings, Controls: every command the mod adds can now be moved to your own keys. Each command is a row of three columns — its name, its primary key, its secondary key — walked with the arrows like any table. `Enter` on a key cell captures the next thing you press; `Delete` empties the cell; `Escape` during a capture cancels it and puts the old key back, saying so. Capturing the key a cell already holds reads the key back like any other capture instead of saying nothing. A "Reset to Defaults" button, the game's own, puts every mod command back where it started, behind the game's own confirmation
- Mod settings, Controls: putting a mod command on a key the game also uses — or a game command on a key the mod uses — is allowed, and said out loud: "While the mod's Move up is active, the game's End Turn will not fire." Confirm keeps the new key; Cancel puts the old one back
